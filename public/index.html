Pesticide Usage Advisory & Organic Farming Awareness

Organic Farming Tips

Build healthy soil

Add compost or well-rotted farmyard manure every season. Healthy soil grows stronger plants that resist pests better.

Rotate your crops

Do not grow the same crop, or its relatives, in the same field year after year. Rotation breaks the life cycle of many soil diseases and pests.

Use neem

Neem oil (3-5 ml per litre of water with a little mild soap) or neem seed kernel extract (5%) controls many soft-bodied insects. Spray in the evening and cover the underside of leaves.

Use traps

Yellow sticky traps catch whitefly and aphids. Blue sticky traps catch thrips. Pheromone traps help you spot borers early.

Protect friendly insects

Ladybird beetles, spiders and parasitic wasps eat pests for free. Broad-spectrum sprays kill them too, so spray only when needed.

Use bio-agents

Trichoderma and Pseudomonas fluorescens help protect roots and seed from soil diseases. Bt sprays target caterpillars.

Grow trap and border crops

Marigold, maize or mustard along field borders can draw pests away from the main crop.

Scout your field

Walk through your field every few days and check the underside of leaves. Catching a problem early often means organic methods are enough.

Pesticide Safety

Before spraying

Read the label and follow the dose exactly. Wear a mask, gloves, full-sleeve clothes and covered shoes. Never spray without protection.

When to spray

Spray in the early morning or evening. Do not spray in strong wind, or when rain is expected soon.

While spraying

Do not eat, drink or smoke. Spray with the wind behind you, never against it.

After spraying

Wash your hands, face and body with soap. Wash the clothes separately. Wait for the number of days on the label before harvesting.

Storage and disposal

Keep pesticides in their original container, locked away from children, food and animals. Never reuse empty containers for food or water.

If someone feels unwell

Move them to fresh air, remove contaminated clothes, wash the skin with soap and water, and take them to the nearest hospital with the pesticide label.
